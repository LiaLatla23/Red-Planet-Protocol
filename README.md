# Red Planet Protocol
### A 2D Mars Mission Design and Exploration Game

Red Planet Protocol is a 2D game in development about designing and operating a robotic mission to Mars.

Players will take part in the mission from the planning stage to surface exploration. They will configure a spacecraft and the ARES-01 rover, prepare their systems for launch, and eventually explore the Jezero Crater region of Mars to investigate geological formations and collect scientific data.

Inspired by real space missions, the game focuses on the engineering decisions and challenges behind robotic exploration.

## The Mission

The goal is to prepare a rover capable of completing its scientific objectives while working within the mission's available resources.

The game will take players through several stages:

1. **Briefing:** Receive the mission objectives and learn about the destination.
2. **Planning:** Review scientific priorities and choose a mission profile.
3. **Design:** Explore the laboratory and configure the spacecraft and rover.
4. **Testing:** Check the design, its limitations, and whether the systems are compatible.
5. **Journey to Mars:** Follow the launch and interplanetary journey.
6. **Surface Exploration:** Control ARES-01, investigate rocks, take photographs, and collect samples.
7. **Mission Report:** Review the discoveries, evaluate the results, and see how earlier decisions affected the mission.

## Designing the Mission

Players will choose components for the rover's structure, mobility, power supply, scientific instruments, computing, storage, and communications.

Every choice will have consequences. The game focuses on six mission resources:

| Resource | What it affects |
|---|---|
| **Mass** | The rover's total weight and transportation requirements. |
| **Power** | Which systems can operate and how long they can run. |
| **Budget** | The resources available for building and operating the mission. |
| **Data** | The information collected and stored by the rover. |
| **Communications** | How and when data can be sent back to Earth. |
| **Time** | How long planning, travel, and surface activities take. |

For example, a more advanced scientific instrument could provide better analysis, but it may also add weight, cost more, consume more power, and generate more data. Players will need to decide which capabilities are worth the trade-offs.

## Exploring Mars

Once the rover reaches Mars, players will take control of ARES-01 and begin exploring the surface.

They will be able to examine geological formations, choose places of interest, photograph rocks, perform analyses, and attempt to collect samples. Not every rock will be equally useful, so players will need to consider what they investigate and why.

Exploration will also involve managing the rover's energy, available storage, and communication opportunities. Collected data will not automatically reach Earth: players will need to account for when and how it can be transmitted.

## Challenges Along the Way

A mission will not always go as planned. Players may encounter difficult terrain, fragile rocks, sample collection problems, limited storage, insufficient power, instrument malfunctions, or communication interruptions.

These situations will require players to understand what went wrong and decide how to respond. Failures should have understandable causes and, where possible, ways to recover.

## Learning Through Gameplay

A mission specialist will guide players through the experience, explaining components and engineering concepts as they become relevant.

Rather than introducing everything in a long tutorial, the guide will provide explanations when players interact with a system or make a decision. Players will be able to see what a component does, how changing it affects the rover, and what trade-offs come with different choices.

The idea is to learn by building, testing, exploring, and making mistakes.

## Mission Results

At the end of the mission, players will receive a report covering more than just whether they succeeded or failed.

The report will evaluate six areas:

- **Scientific Return:** The value of the discoveries.
- **Mission Success:** How many objectives were completed.
- **Budget Performance:** How the available budget was used.
- **Schedule Performance:** How the mission followed its planned timeline.
- **Resource Efficiency:** What was achieved with the resources available.
- **Data Return:** How much useful scientific data reached Earth.

It will also explain how important decisions influenced the outcome and what could be improved in another mission.

## Development

Red Planet Protocol is being developed around a focused first mission. The initial goal is to bring the main experience together rather than build a large game with too many unfinished features.

The planned first version will focus on mission planning, spacecraft and rover design, system validation, the journey to Mars, rover exploration, sample collection, resource management, and a final mission report.

Advanced orbital simulations, multiple destinations, extensive research progression, and larger component catalogs may be considered for future versions.

**The project is currently a work in progress.** Some mechanics and technical details are still being designed, so the final gameplay may change during development.
